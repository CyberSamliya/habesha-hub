Lesson 1

Alert Triage Fundamentals

Goal: learn how a SOC analyst turns an alert into a reasoned decision.

1. Read the alert.
Identify who, what, when, where, and why it fired.
2. Validate context.
Is the activity expected for this user, host, or application?
3. Examine evidence.
Review log details before deciding whether the alert is benign or suspicious.
4. Classify.
Choose True Positive, False Positive, or Needs Investigation.
5. Document.
Record evidence and the reasoning behind your decision.

Why this matters

Most SOC work is not just reading alerts. The analyst must separate normal behavior from meaningful risk and leave a defensible record of the decision.

Today’s scenario

An administrator account has several failed sign-ins, followed by one successful sign-in. Your task is to determine whether this looks like a typo, password spraying, credential stuffing, or another suspicious pattern.

What to look for

  • Source IP consistency
  • Timing between failures
  • Whether the successful login came from the same source
  • User agent changes
  • Whether the pattern repeats
